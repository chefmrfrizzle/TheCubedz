# Thesis and research program

## The defensible thesis

TheCubedz should not claim that artificial intelligence can solve spacetime or decide which geometries are physically possible.

The research thesis is:

> Can a provenance-preserving, adversarial, benchmark-gated computational research system select and execute more informative spacetime evaluations than transparent baselines, while preventing models from promoting their own scientific claims?

This thesis is ambitious, measurable, and compatible with the present evidence. It makes the research instrument itself the first object of study. A future physics result is possible, but it is not assumed.

## Why the cube matters

The cube is not a claim that physics has six literal sides. It is an interface for inspecting one candidate through six independent assessment faces:

1. **Definition** - coordinates, metric, units, domain, assumptions, and provenance.
2. **Mathematics** - exact identities and derived quantities implemented by named validators.
3. **Numerics** - tolerance, convergence, conditioning, and solver agreement.
4. **Physics** - stress-energy requirements, energy conditions, stability, and causality.
5. **Reproduction** - environment, implementation, solver, and reviewer independence.
6. **Realizability** - compatibility with known matter, experimental evidence, and engineering constraints.

Each face contains checks with separate states: `PASS`, `FAIL`, `UNRESOLVED`, `UNSUPPORTED`, or `NOT_APPLICABLE`. The cube must never collapse these into a universal score. Rotating it changes the view, not the evidence.

## Primary research questions

### RQ1 - Instrument validity

Can the system recover known analytic properties and known failure modes without overstating validator scope?

### RQ2 - Experiment-selection efficiency

On a frozen benchmark and equal compute budget, can a versioned search policy select evaluations that reduce uncertainty or resolve contradictions faster than random, grid, and hand-written baselines?

### RQ3 - Reproducibility

Can independent environments and implementations recover the same declared scientific payload, with discrepancies preserved rather than normalized away?

### RQ4 - Explanation fidelity

Can beginner, practitioner, and technical explanations preserve the same claims, limitations, and falsification conditions as the source artifact?

### RQ5 - Safe public participation

Can outside contributors propose questions, candidates, challenges, and reproduction jobs without executing arbitrary code, accessing secrets, or changing canonical scientific status?

## Falsifiable program hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | Required comparison | Failure condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | The validation pipeline detects declared malformed and contradictory inputs. | Frozen synthetic suite v1 | Any required case is silently accepted, silently changed, or misclassified. |
| H2 | A second implementation reproduces the Minkowski baseline. | Current validator versus independent implementation | Scientific payload differs and the discrepancy cannot be explained and recorded. |
| H3 | The public cube improves evidence comprehension. | Usability study against a flat result page | Users more often confuse `PASS` with physical or engineering feasibility. |
| H4 | An active-falsification policy is more informative than naive selection. | Equal-budget random, grid, and hand-written baselines | It fails preregistered information-gain, calibration, or cost thresholds. |
| H5 | Controlled contribution prevents unauthorized promotion. | Security and abuse-case suite | A public input can change canonical status, escape execution limits, or expose a credential. |

Thresholds for H3 and H4 must be preregistered before collecting evaluation data. They must not be selected after results are visible.

## Publishable outputs that do not require new physics

- A versioned benchmark and failure atlas for scientific-workflow integrity.
- A reproducibility-passport format and cross-implementation comparison method.
- A study of falsification-oriented experiment selection against transparent baselines.
- A study of whether multidimensional evidence displays reduce claim overstatement.
- A security and governance pattern for agent-assisted public science.

These outputs can be independently reviewed even if no novel spacetime candidate survives.

## Claims that remain prohibited

- The AI solved spacetime.
- The cube proves that a candidate is physically possible.
- Agreement among agents is independent evidence.
- Passing the synthetic suite validates a new physical theory.
- A search-policy win proves scientific novelty.
- A mathematically consistent result is buildable or useful for transportation.

## Program order

1. Repair release integrity and make the full check cross-platform.
2. Add typed task, run, evidence, and promotion contracts.
3. Complete the 100-case synthetic workflow benchmark.
4. Add an independent baseline validator and one outside reproduction record.
5. Build the evidence cube from committed artifacts.
6. Open structured, non-executable public contribution flows.
7. Evaluate search policies on a frozen benchmark.
8. Consider bounded candidate generation only after the earlier gates pass.
